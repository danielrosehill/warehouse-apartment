# Interoperability: The Global Standards

This is the chapter I most wish someone had handed me before I started. If you've
never worked in or around logistics, the single most useful thing to understand is
that industrial storage is not a pile of random products — it's a **small number of
interlocking dimensional standards**, and once you know them, everything in the
ecosystem fits everything else on purpose.

The catch worth stating up front: **the Eurobox is only one standard in a wider
system, and the systems differ by region.** If you're moving in Europe vs. North
America vs. elsewhere, you'll be buying into a different (but internally consistent)
grid. This page is the map.

## The core idea: modular dimensional grids

Every mature logistics region converges on a **base module** and builds boxes,
shelving, and pallets as clean fractions and multiples of it. Stay on the grid and
things tile with zero wasted space. Go off-grid (most consumer products) and you
get gaps, wobble, and wasted volume.

## Pallet standards (the top of the stack)

The pallet is the master unit almost everything else is sized against.

| Standard | Size | Region / use |
|----------|------|--------------|
| **Euro pallet (EPAL / EUR)** | **1200 × 800 mm** | Europe. The one Euroboxes are built to divide. |
| **Industrial / EUR-2 ("block") pallet** | **1200 × 1000 mm** | Europe/global industry, heavier loads. |
| **North American (GMA) pallet** | **48 × 40 in (~1219 × 1016 mm)** | USA/Canada standard. |
| **Australian standard pallet** | **1165 × 1165 mm** | Australia (square). |
| **ISO pallets** | six sizes incl. all of the above | The international umbrella (ISO 6780). |

**Practical takeaway:** if you're in Europe, everything you buy should be sized to
tile onto **1200 × 800**. In North America the reference is **48 × 40 in**. Pick
your region's pallet first; it determines everything below it.

## Box footprints (the middle layer)

The **Euro container** system (EN 13199 family / "KLT") is standardized on a
**600 × 400 mm** footprint and its divisions. It divides the 1200 × 800 Euro pallet
exactly, which is the whole point (see the tiling note below).

Clean divisions of the 600 × 400 module:

| Footprint (mm) | Fraction of 600×400 |
|----------------|---------------------|
| 600 × 400 | 1/1 |
| 400 × 300 | 1/2 |
| 300 × 200 | 1/4 |
| 200 × 150 | 1/8 |

Tiling upward: a **1200 × 800 Euro pallet** holds exactly **4 × 600 × 400
footprints** per layer (1200/600 × 800/400 = 2 × 2). No wasted space. Verify the
arithmetic for your own footprints before buying — clean division is the whole
value of the system.

## Shelving depth (the quiet standard)

Here's one almost nobody outside the trade knows: **industrial shelving is
standardized to the same depths as the boxes** — very commonly **600 mm deep** (and
300/400 mm variants). That's not a coincidence; the shelving is built so a Eurobox
sits on it with its full footprint supported and its face flush to the aisle.

**Practical takeaway:** buy shelving in the depth that matches your box footprint
(e.g. 600 mm shelving for 600 × 400 boxes) and your storage wall becomes a single
coordinated system instead of boxes-perched-on-random-shelves.

## Adjacent standards worth knowing

- **Gastronorm (GN)** — the kitchen equivalent; see [Gastronorm](gastronorm.md).
- **ISO shipping containers** — the top of the whole chain; see

- **19-inch rack** — the same "pick a standard and everything fits" logic, for
  electronics, if you ever go down that road.

## The one-paragraph version for a mover

Pick your **region's pallet** (Europe: 1200 × 800; North America: 48 × 40 in).
Buy **boxes whose footprint divides it cleanly** (in Europe: the 600 × 400 Euro
grid). Buy **shelving in the matching depth**. Now your boxes, shelves, and pallet
are one interoperable system — you can stack, shelve, and palletize without a single
wasted centimeter, and you can do it again in your next apartment, or your next
country, because the standard travels with you.
