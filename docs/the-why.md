# The Why

This system wasn't a hobby. It's a response to a specific set of conditions in the
Israeli rental market that make frequent, involuntary moving both likely and
financially brutal. This page explains those conditions — because they're the
reason the rest of the repo exists — and then hands off to the system itself.

> **Note:** the legal points below are a layperson's summary of my own experience as
> a long-term renter and immigrant. Verify specifics against current law before
> relying on them.

## The situation, briefly

Renting in Israel ~10 years, always paid in full, displaced repeatedly for reasons
unrelated to us — one landlord gave the flat to his daughter; another terminated our
tenancy after we asked him to fix a leak. This isn't bad luck. It's how the market
is structured.

## The mechanics: why moves are forced *and* expensive

### 1. No security of tenure
- Residential leases are typically **fixed 12-month terms**. At the end, the
  landlord is generally **free not to renew** — no cause required, roughly a month's
  notice in practice.
- Israel has **no general rent control** and, compared to much of Europe, weak
  security of tenure.
- The main reform, the **Fair Rental Law, 2017** (חוק שכירות הוגנת — an amendment to
  the *Rental and Lending Law, 1971*, חוק השכירות והשאילה), set some habitability
  minimums and capped the security deposit, but it **does not give tenants a right to
  renew.** The core vulnerability — being asked to leave at term's end — remains.

### 2. The move triggers a stack of costs at once
A single forced move can put a household under the equivalent of **~3 months' rent in
one hit**, before rent on the new place even starts:

| Cost | Typical magnitude | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| **Security / deposit** | up to **~3 months' rent** | Bank guarantee (ערבות בנקאית), deposit check, promissory note, and/or guarantors. The 2017 law caps the security at the lower of 3 months' rent or 1/3 of the lease value — a *ceiling*, not a low number. |
| **Agent / broker fee** | **1 month's rent + VAT** | Under the Real Estate Brokers Law, 1996. Commonly charged to the tenant; whether the tenant should bear it at all is contested, but the market enforces it. |
| **Moving cost** | e.g. **~10,000 ₪** (our prior move) | Movers, or DIY gear + labour. |
| **Lost income** | variable | If you're **self-employed** (we are), the days spent packing/moving/setting up are unpaid. |

Add these up and an ordinary move can mean **~3 months' rent in deposits + ~1 month
in agent fee + the move itself + lost work** — landing all at once, at the moment a
family is least able to absorb it.

### 3. Comparison: jurisdictions that handle this better
The point isn't that moving is hard everywhere. It's that some places have designed
the costs out:

| Place | Security of tenure | Tenant-paid broker fee |
|-------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **Germany** | Often **indefinite** leases; non-renewal requires legal grounds | **Banned** for tenants — *Bestellerprinzip*: whoever hires the agent pays (usually the landlord) |
| **Much of the EU** | Longer terms / stronger non-renewal protection common | Frequently capped or restricted |
| **Israel** | Fixed 12-month terms, no right to renew | ~1 month + VAT, commonly on the tenant |

Same activity, very different exposure. In Israel the structure pushes the full
financial and logistical risk of the market's instability onto the tenant.

## The response

You can't fix the law from your living room. But you *can* change what a move costs
**you** — in money, time, and back pain — by borrowing the tools industry already
built for moving things efficiently and repeatedly.

That's the rest of this repository. The belongings live in standardized, interlocking
industrial containers that double as moving boxes and permanent storage; those tile
onto pallets; pallets move on jacks. Each move gets cheaper and less painful than the
last, because the "boxes" already exist and already hold our lives.
